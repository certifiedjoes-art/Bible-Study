How this works

Each sitting covers one chapter or a short passage. Read it twice — once straight through, once slowly with a pencil — then break it into sections and fill in what you found in each one.

  1. 1
    Read it twice.First for the shape of the passage, second to mark it up.
  2. 2
    Split it into sections and mark the four things.Starting from the top of the chapter, title each section (a verse range, a scene, a speech), then note its observations (who, what, when, what came before), geography (every place named — look each one up), promises (what God says He will do), and commands (what we're told to do, and to whom).
  3. 3
    Summarize in one sentence.In your own words, not the text's. If you can't, you haven't found the center yet.
  4. 4
    Write down one open question.Something you couldn't answer. Come back to it as later chapters fill it in.

Aim for 20–30 minutes, four or five days a week. One book at a time beats a whole-Bible plan — the Findings tab collects your timeline, gazetteer, promises and commands automatically as you go, so the big picture builds itself.

Suggested order: Genesis (foundations, first promises) → Exodus (deliverance, the law) → Mark (a fast, short Gospel) → Acts (the church begins). Add or reorder books from the Study tab any time.
